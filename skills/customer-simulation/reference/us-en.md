# United States

## What breaks on entry

Playbooks travel badly. These are the habits that break when a startup takes its home-market approach into the next market.

- Treating the legal minimum as the standard for returns. Free returns are expected.
- WhatsApp-first communication: 32% of US adults use it, against 90% in the UK.
- One national rule set. Subscription rules differ by state.
- Invoice or direct-debit checkout. US payments run on cards.

## B2B: how this market buys

- **Verify the pain:** Giving back has a name here. In Silicon Valley it is called paying it forward: people who were helped at the start help the next founder (Steve Blank). Writing to a stranger is accepted. 31% of adults use LinkedIn (Pew 2026).
- **Position: wedge and vision:** Win before the first call. Worldwide, buyers first talk to a seller when about 61% of their buying process is behind them, and a purchase in North America takes about 11 months (6sense 2025, for purchases of typically $200,000 to $300,000). The positioning has to work on the website, in reviews and in AI answers.
- **Offer: free trial, clear path to paid:** Month to month is accepted. Most trial and pricing figures in this step are US data. Datadog sold month to month for a long time and avoided annual contracts, so that cancellations showed problems fast.
- **Decide how you sell:** The often-quoted 11 months are for big purchases. 6sense measures 11.1 months for North America (2025), but the purchases in that study are typically worth $200,000 to $300,000. Across B2B software companies of all sizes the sales cycle is about 19 weeks (ICONIQ 2026). Under $25,000 a year, plan with the three ways above.
- **First customers through networks:** Cold outreach is a real option here. US law allows cold email to businesses as long as there is a working opt-out. Alex Hormozi counts cold outreach among the four core ways to get leads and recommends 100 contacts a day. The numbers: cold emails get a reply in 3.43% of cases on average and in over 10.7% for the best tenth (Instantly 2026). Cold calls succeed 2.7% of the time on average, a strong team reaches 11.3% (Cognism 2026). It works through volume and skill. Run it next to the network.
- **Channel mix: AI, founder, networks:** Widest choice of founder platforms. YouTube 84%, Instagram 50%, TikTok 37%, LinkedIn 31% of adults (Pew 2025 and 2026).
- **Scale what works:** One language, one large market. Wiz, Snowflake and Datadog scaled in the US market first. Partners multiply reach.

## B2C: how this market buys

- **Verify the pain:** The largest single-language market. Most published advice was written for it. Narrow by community, not by country.
- **Build a love brand:** Word of mouth travels on open platforms. YouTube 84%, Facebook 71%, Instagram 50% of adults. WhatsApp only 32% (Pew 2025).
- **Offer: free trial, clear path to paid:** Rules differ by state. The federal click-to-cancel rule was struck down in July 2025. California requires cancellation through the same channel as sign-up since July 2025.
- **First 1,000 by hand, then evangelists:** Do not build the community on WhatsApp. Only 32% of adults use it. YouTube 84%, Facebook 71%, Instagram 50%, TikTok 37% (Pew 2025).
- **Prove organic growth:** Cards, and free returns. There is no federal right to return online purchases, yet 19.3% of online sales come back and 82% say free returns matter (NRF 2025).
- **Channel mix: AI, founder, where people shop:** Social shops are a channel of their own. The largest social-commerce market, with TikTok Shop at nearly 20% of it (eMarketer 2025).
- **Amplify: channels, incentives, community:** Returns are part of the offer. 19.3% of online sales come back and 82% say free returns matter (NRF 2025). Test TikTok Shop as a channel of its own.
