# DACH (Germany, Austria, Switzerland)

## What breaks on entry

Playbooks travel badly. These are the habits that break when a startup takes its home-market approach into the next market.

- Cold email to businesses without prior consent. Build the network instead.
- Card-only checkout. PayPal, invoice and direct debit make up about 69% of German online revenue.
- English-only website and "#ad" labels.
- Discounts without the 30-day lowest price. Subscriptions without the cancel button.
- Digital-only B2B without trade fairs. Plain PDF invoices.
- Big claims without local proof.

## B2B: how this market buys

- **Verify the pain:** Ask for advice in German, through LinkedIn and introductions. A request for advice lands best when someone introduces you. Germany restricts advertising by email without prior consent (UWG §7), so keep the invitation free of any offer. 57% of German consumers buy only from websites in their own language (CSA 2020, as an indication).
- **Position: wedge and vision:** Reduce risk, do not promise the moon. In Germany the vendor ranked first before any contact wins 77% of deals, and 84% of buyers make the first contact themselves (6sense 2025). Claims like "market-leading" meet scepticism unless proven at once. Build German case studies and German-language reviews in the first year (OMR 2026).
- **Offer: free trial, clear path to paid:** Do not end the trial in a card-only checkout. Only 35% of German B2B sales are made on credit and 87% of companies report late payments (Atradius 2026). SEPA direct debit and bank transfer are common, cards less so (Stripe 2026). Invoices must be structured e-invoices; a plain PDF no longer counts (German Ministry of Finance 2026).
- **Decide how you sell:** Large deals mean large groups. For purchases in the mid-to-high $200,000s, German buying groups have 11 people, compare 5.3 vendors and take 11 months, up from 9 people and 9 months a year earlier (6sense 2025). Germany and the Netherlands have the largest groups in the European study. Belgium, France and Sweden are at 7 to 7.5 people.
- **First customers through networks:** Here the network is not optional. Advertising by email needs prior consent. A call to a business needs at least presumed consent (UWG §7). Trade fairs are the largest existing networks: 322 fairs and 11.7m visitors in 2024 (AUMA). Host your dinner on the evening of the fair. 84% of German buyers make the first contact themselves (6sense 2025).
- **Channel mix: AI, founder, networks:** The AI channel is mainstream, and it wants German sources. 50% of German internet users use AI chats at least sometimes instead of a search engine (Bitkom 2025), and 94% of German business buyers use AI assistants in their research (6sense 2025). Assistants answer German questions from German-language pages (OMR 2026). YouTube reaches 71% and Spotify 44% of the population (ARD/ZDF 2026): video and podcasts in German are open lanes.
- **Scale what works:** Two proven ways from Munich. Celonis grew through consultancies and a focus on industries, Personio with a 400-person sales team. Each matched its way of selling to its customer.

## B2C: how this market buys

- **Verify the pain:** Three countries, one language, local habits. 57% of German consumers buy only from websites in their own language (CSA 2020), although English skills are high (EF 2026). Run panels in German. The German provider Appinio prices per participant, depending on target group and survey length.
- **Build a love brand:** Speak German and prove it. 57% of German consumers buy only in their own language (CSA 2020). Reviews count: positive reviews are the top factor that turns discovery into purchase (Clutch 2026).
- **Offer: free trial, clear path to paid:** Cancel button and honest discounts. In Germany subscriptions need a permanent cancel button (BGB §312k). An advertised reduction must show the lowest price of the last 30 days (PAngV §11).
- **First 1,000 by hand, then evangelists:** Label what evangelists get rewarded for. YouTube reaches 71% in Germany (ARD/ZDF 2026). When evangelists or creators receive something in return, posts must be labelled "Werbung" or "Anzeige" at the start (die medienanstalten).
- **Prove organic growth:** PayPal, invoice, direct debit. Share of German online revenue: PayPal 28.7%, invoice 26.1%, direct debit 14.4%, cards 13.7% (EHI 2026). In Switzerland TWINT handles 67% of mobile payments at a distance. Nearly one in four parcels was returned in 2025.
- **Channel mix: AI, founder, where people shop:** Own shop only is a plan against the majority. Marketplaces carried 56.7% of German online revenue in 2025 (HDE 2026). 50% of German internet users use AI chats at least sometimes instead of a search engine (Bitkom 2025). TikTok Shop only since March 2025.
- **Amplify: channels, incentives, community:** Put marketplaces into the test. They carry 56.7% of German online revenue (HDE 2026). Advertised price reductions must show the lowest price of the last 30 days (PAngV §11). Affiliate and creator posts need the "Werbung" or "Anzeige" label.
